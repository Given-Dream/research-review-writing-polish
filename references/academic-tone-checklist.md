# Academic Tone Checklist

This file helps keep the prose clear, formal, credible, and reviewer-friendly.
It is based on Wallwork's repeated principles across sentence clarity, paragraph structure, concision, ambiguity control, highlighting, limitation handling, readability, and final checking.

## Core idea

Academic tone is not produced by sounding complicated.
Academic tone comes from:
- clarity
- control
- precision
- proportionality
- evidential honesty
- reader awareness

## Use when

Use this file when the user asks to:
- polish language
- make text more academic
- reduce informality
- increase readability
- check tone before submission
- prevent overclaiming or vagueness

## Primary checklist

## 1. Clarity

Ask:
- Is each sentence easy to parse on first reading?
- Is the subject close to the verb?
- Is the verb close to its object where possible?
- Have long nested structures been reduced?
- Are important points buried in long sentences?

Prefer:
- direct sentence structure
- shorter sentences for important findings
- paragraph openings that orient the reader quickly

## 2. Concision

Ask:
- Does each sentence add value?
- Can generic filler be removed?
- Can a noun-heavy phrase become a simpler verb phrase?
- Is the same idea being said twice?

Prefer:
- concrete wording
- shorter alternatives when meaning is unchanged
- deletion of empty lead-ins and redundant modifiers

Avoid:
- `it is important to note that`
- `interesting`, `remarkable`, `novel` without explanation
- long openings that delay the main point

## 3. Precision

Ask:
- Is the wording specific enough for the reader to understand what is meant?
- Are technical terms used consistently?
- Are pronouns always unambiguous?
- Does each evaluative word have support?

Prefer:
- exact variables, effects, comparisons, and contexts
- repeating a key noun when a pronoun would create confusion
- concrete detail instead of praise words

## 4. Contribution visibility

Ask:
- Can the reader easily locate what is new?
- Is the gap clearly visible?
- Is the contribution distinguishable from prior work?

Prefer:
- explicit contrast between previous work and present work
- visible statement of contribution in high-value sections
- direct explanation of why the result matters

Avoid:
- assuming the reader will infer novelty
- praising contribution without stating what changed

## 5. Evidential discipline

Ask:
- Does every claim stay within the evidence?
- Have results been turned into broader claims too quickly?
- Is causality stronger than the study design allows?
- Are alternative interpretations or limitations being suppressed?

Prefer:
- cautious verbs when needed
- explicit distinction between finding and interpretation
- honest mention of limitations and negative results

Avoid:
- over-interpretation
- one-sided presentation
- hiding contradictory or null results

## 6. Reader guidance

Ask:
- Does the paragraph tell the reader why the information is here?
- Are transitions clear enough to signal contrast, consequence, or next step?
- Is it obvious whether the text is discussing the author's work or prior literature?

Prefer:
- clear paragraph progression
- mini-summaries at natural points
- visible transitions between subtopics
- explicit markers when switching from others' work to your own

## 7. Formality

Ask:
- Is the register suitable for a journal paper?
- Are contractions, casual adjectives, and conversational fillers removed?
- Is the wording professional without becoming inflated?

Prefer:
- formal but readable prose
- measured tone
- discipline-appropriate terminology

Avoid:
- slang
- chatty framing
- exaggerated certainty
- decorative complexity

## 8. Readability

Ask:
- Are sentences mostly manageable in length?
- Are paragraphs visually and logically digestible?
- Is there too much abstraction?
- Are figures and tables interpreted rather than repeated?

Prefer:
- white space through reasonable paragraph length
- one paragraph, one local job
- interpretation over table repetition
- text that does not force the reader to hold too much unresolved information

## 9. Consistency

Ask:
- Are terms used the same way throughout?
- Is spelling style consistent?
- Are citation and capitalization patterns consistent?
- Do table/figure references match the text?
- Are section-level claims consistent with data and captions?

## 10. AI-use caution

If AI is used for generation or polishing:
- treat output as a draft, not authority
- verify specificity, tense, and factual alignment
- remove generic endings and vague implications
- do not submit unedited generated text
- do not use AI paraphrase to hide source borrowing

## Quick diagnosis by symptom

### If the prose sounds dull

- shorten sentences
- move key findings earlier
- remove generic wording
- add explicit contrasts where relevant
- replace labels like `interesting` with the evidence that makes it interesting

### If the prose sounds inflated

- remove hype adjectives
- narrow the claim
- replace abstract praise with precise consequence
- add limitation or scope markers

### If the prose sounds thesis-like

- cut secondary detail
- prioritize representative findings
- reduce exhaustive reporting
- increase section signaling

### If the prose sounds vague

- name the object, variable, comparison, or condition
- give the reason or implication explicitly
- replace pronouns or broad nouns with precise terms

## Final pre-submission pass

Before treating a section as finished, ask:
- Would a referee understand this without rereading?
- Does it respect the reader's time?
- Is the tone clear rather than pompous?
- Is the claim strong enough to matter but not stronger than the evidence?
- Have I cut everything that does not earn its place?
