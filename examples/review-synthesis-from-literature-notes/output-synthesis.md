# Output Synthesis

Research on this topic appears to have moved through two related phases. Early work was primarily performance-oriented, with studies emphasizing classification accuracy under controlled conditions. For example, Wang (2020) reported strong predictive performance, but offered little support for explaining how the model reached its decisions. More recent studies have shifted attention toward interpretability and user trust, although they do not yet converge on what meaningful interpretability should look like in practice. Ibrahim (2021) introduced saliency-based explanations, yet clinicians reportedly found these outputs unstable, suggesting that the presence of an explanation module does not in itself guarantee usability. This concern is developed further by Kumar (2022), who argued that interpretability should be assessed with end users rather than through technical metrics alone. In a similar direction, Li (2023) showed that clinicians valued consistency more strongly than visually complex explanation interfaces. Taken together, these studies indicate a clear shift from performance-first evaluation to usability-aware model design, but they also reveal that the field still lacks a stable framework for assessing interpretability within real clinical workflows.
