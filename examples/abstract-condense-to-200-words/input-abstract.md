# Input Abstract

This study investigates the relationship between sleep quality, screen exposure at night, and next-day concentration in first-year university students. Although previous studies have reported associations between digital device use and sleep disruption, relatively few have examined how these factors interact during the transition to university life. We collected weekly survey data from 186 first-year students over an eight-week period and combined these data with short daily concentration self-ratings. Sleep quality was measured using a five-item self-report scale, while screen exposure after 10 p.m. was recorded in minutes. The analysis showed that higher night-time screen exposure was associated with poorer reported sleep quality, and poorer sleep quality was in turn associated with lower next-day concentration ratings. The indirect pathway appeared stronger than the direct association between screen exposure and concentration. These findings suggest that sleep quality may play an important mediating role in the relationship between late-night screen use and academic functioning in early university adjustment. However, the study relied on self-reported measures and was conducted within a single institution. Future research should therefore examine whether similar patterns are observed using objective sleep measures and more diverse student populations.
