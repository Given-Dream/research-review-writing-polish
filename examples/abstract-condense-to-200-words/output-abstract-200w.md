# Output Abstract

This study examined the relationship between late-night screen exposure, sleep quality, and next-day concentration in first-year university students. While previous research has linked digital device use to sleep disruption, less attention has been paid to how these factors interact during the transition to university life. Weekly survey data were collected from 186 first-year students over eight weeks and combined with daily self-ratings of concentration. Sleep quality was assessed using a five-item self-report scale, and screen exposure after 10 p.m. was recorded in minutes. Higher night-time screen exposure was associated with poorer reported sleep quality, and poorer sleep quality was in turn associated with lower next-day concentration ratings. The indirect relationship through sleep quality appeared stronger than the direct association between screen exposure and concentration. These findings suggest that sleep quality may be an important pathway linking late-night screen use to academic functioning during early university adjustment. However, the study was based on self-reported measures from a single institution. Further research is needed to determine whether the same pattern can be confirmed using objective sleep measures and more diverse student populations.
