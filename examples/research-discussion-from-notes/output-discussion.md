# Output Discussion

The present findings suggest that structured peer feedback may be more effective than teacher comments alone in improving revision quality, particularly in relation to organization and coherence. This pattern is broadly consistent with earlier work reporting that peer-supported revision can strengthen higher-order aspects of writing more readily than sentence-level accuracy (Lee, 2021; Santos, 2023). One possible explanation is that peer feedback encouraged students to re-read their drafts more actively and to evaluate their texts in comparative terms, thereby making structural weaknesses more visible during revision. However, the effect observed here was less pronounced for grammatical accuracy, which may indicate that peer review is more useful for global text-level revision than for local language correction. These conclusions should nevertheless be interpreted with caution, as the study involved only 48 students from a single department and the intervention lasted just six weeks. Accordingly, while the results support the pedagogical value of structured peer feedback in short-term revision tasks, they do not yet justify broader claims regarding sustained improvement in writing ability over time.
